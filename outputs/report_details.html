How the numbers work

Wording spread: highest minus lowest mean score across the original and its same-meaning variants (1–10 scale). Noise: the spread pure run-to-run randomness would produce for that many wordings: the standard error of one run's mean (from per-persona answers, or pooled original-vs-repeat gaps) × the expected range of k normal draws (2.33 for k=5). WSS = spread ÷ noise. A concept is unstable when WSS ≥ 1.5, the spread is at least 1.0 point (big enough to change a decision), and a permutation test (answers shuffled across wordings, 2,000 times) gives p < 0.05. Multiple testing: 16 concepts were tested at once, so about one could pass p < 0.05 by chance. With a Benjamini-Hochberg correction (false discovery rate 0.05), 4 of the 8 unstable concepts still hold: C03, C06, C07, C13. The "BH p" column shows each concept's adjusted p.

Prediction: for batch 2, Claude writes the variants and tags their features, and the rules from batch 1 predict each concept's spread before any panel run. "No memory" predicts no wording effects, so it flags nothing.

Limits: 8+8 concepts is a proof of method, not a benchmark. Stable is not the same as correct: this tool says when a synthetic answer depends on wording, not whether real people agree.
